In-Depth Q&A: Tariq's Full Interview

Q: What went through your mind on A-Level results day, and why did you look into Europe?

“I had AAB in Biology, Chemistry, and Maths. While those are competitive grades, the UK admissions system is so oversubscribed that my conditional offers fell through. Taking a gap year to re-sit with no guarantee of entry sounded exhausting. When I came across GlobalMed Entry Ltd and spoke directly with Dr. Samuel—an NHS doctor who had taken the exact same route through MU Sofia—all my doubts evaporated. Knowing someone had already walked the path and was thriving in the NHS made the decision obvious.”

Q: How did GlobalMed Entry Ltd prepare you for the Sofia entrance exam?

“The prep course was exceptional. GlobalMed Entry provided past papers spanning the previous 8 years, broken down topic-by-topic for Biology and Chemistry. They ran mock exams under timed conditions and held tutorial webinars explaining the exact European MCQ question style. When exam day arrived, there were literally no surprises—I scored 96% and received my formal acceptance letter within days.”

Q: What was student life in Sofia like, and how was the language barrier?

“Sofia is a modern, vibrant European capital with thousands of British, Irish, and German medical students. The cost of living is astonishingly affordable—my modern one-bedroom apartment near South Park was around €380 per month. Academic teaching is 100% in English. The university also teaches you conversational Bulgarian in the first two years, which was more than enough to take patient histories when we started hospital rotations.”

Q: How did you find the clinical rotations and patient contact?

“One big advantage of European medical schools is the sheer volume of hands-on patient exposure. In the UK, students often observe from the back of the ward round. In Sofia, at Aleksandrovska Hospital, we were doing physical exams, assisting in the operating theatre, drawing arterial blood gases, and suturing lacerations from year 3. When I started as an FY1 doctor in London, I felt significantly more prepared for bedside clinical emergencies than many of my UK peers.”

Q: How was the UKMLA and returning to the NHS after graduation?

“GlobalMed Entry’s Phase IV Alumni team was essential here. They verified my degree through EPIC, helped package my Certificate of Good Standing, and guided my UKMLA AKT revision. I passed the AKT on my first attempt and was matched directly to the East London Foundation School rotation at the Royal London Hospital. The GMC registration process was smooth and predictable.”

Q: What is your advice to parents and students who might be on the fence?

“Do not let pride or fear hold you back from your calling. If you want to be a doctor, studying in Europe is not a compromise—it is an incredible adventure that produces resilient, highly capable clinicians. And most importantly, partner with an agency like GlobalMed Entry Ltd that is founded by doctors and actually maintains staff on the ground throughout your degree.”
